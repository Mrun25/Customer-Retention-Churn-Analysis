## 1. Business Context

Customer acquisition is costly, and sustainable business growth depends on retaining existing customers and encouraging repeat purchases. Many organizations, however, lack clear visibility into *when* and *why* customers stop purchasing and how retention behavior evolves over time.

This project uses **pure Python-based data analysis** to study customer retention patterns, identify churn behavior, and quantify the revenue impact of repeat customers. The objective is to generate actionable, data-driven insights that can help businesses improve retention strategies and increase Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

---

## 2. Business Problems Addressed

### Customer Retention

* How many customers return to make repeat purchases?
* How frequently do customers purchase after their first order?
* How does retention change across different customer cohorts?

### Churn Identification

* When should a customer be considered churned in the absence of explicit labels?
* How long do customers typically remain active before stopping purchases?
* What proportion of customers can be classified as churned?

### Revenue Contribution

* How much revenue is generated by repeat customers versus one-time buyers?
* Which customer groups contribute most to long-term revenue?

## 3. Dataset Overview

**Source:** Kaggle — Olist Brazilian E-commerce Dataset

**Datasets Used**

* Customers dataset
* Orders dataset
* Order payments dataset

**Key Fields**

* `customer_unique_id`: Unique identifier for customers
* `order_id`: Unique identifier for orders
* `order_purchase_timestamp`: Timestamp of order purchase
* `payment_value`: Total payment value of an order

The dataset represents real-world transactional behavior from a large Brazilian e-commerce platform.

## 6. Retention & Churn Definitions

### Repeat Customer

A customer who has placed **more than one completed order**.

### Churn (Proxy Definition)

A customer is considered **churned** if:

> Days since last purchase > 90 days

This proxy reflects common industry practice when explicit churn labels are unavailable.

---

## 7. Analysis Methodology

### First-Time vs Repeat Buyers

* Customers were segmented based on total completed orders
* Repeat purchase rate was calculated

### Time Between Purchases

* Purchase intervals were computed using order timestamps
* Distribution of days between consecutive purchases was analyzed

### Cohort Analysis

* Customers were grouped by their first purchase month
* Month-over-month retention was tracked for each cohort

### Revenue Contribution Analysis

* Revenue was segmented into one-time and repeat customers
* Revenue contribution percentages were calculated and compared

---

## 8. Key Insights

* A large proportion of customers make only a single purchase
* Retention drops sharply after the first purchase window
* Repeat customers contribute a disproportionately high share of total revenue
* Most churn occurs within the first few months after the initial purchase

## 11. Limitations & Future Improvements

### Limitations

* Churn is defined using a time-based proxy rather than explicit labels
* No demographic, marketing, or product-level features were included

### Future Improvements

* Segment retention by geography or product category
* Implement Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) modeling
* Apply survival analysis for more robust churn prediction
